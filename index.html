Apple Season

By Katie Wagner

An apple hangs
from the thinnest branch,
red like a ruby.

I reach for it
and the tree bends.
Leaves shake above me,
but the apple is still.

Pull.

Twist.

Snap.

Suddenly it is mine,
cool and heavy in my hand.
I polish it against my sleeve
until the skin shines.

The first bite is loud.
Sweet,
then sour,
then sweet again.

In a moment,
only the core is left.
I leave it beneath the tree,
the scent stays on my hands.
